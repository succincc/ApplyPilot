1. EVERYTHING IN ONE PLACE, MOBILE ONLY  —  SOLVED, BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The blocker was the Chrome extension: Chrome on Android/iOS does not support
extensions, so "mobile only" and "the extension applies" could not both be
true.

The fix is a cloud browser. GitHub Actions runs Playwright — a real Chromium —
on their machines, free:

    public repositories    unlimited runner minutes
    private repositories   2,000 minutes/month  (~1,000 applications)

At roughly 1.5-2 minutes per application, a PUBLIC repo gives you unlimited
applications at zero cost. And nothing personal needs to live in that repo:
the resume, profile and queue are all pulled from Supabase at runtime using a
secret. The repo holds only the automation code, so public is safe.

  BUILT:  cloud-agent/apply.mjs
          cloud-agent/.github/workflows/apply.yml

  Triggers three ways: a button in your panel (repository_dispatch), every 3
  hours on a schedule, or by hand from the Actions tab.

  ONE THING THE CLOUD AGENT DOES THAT THE EXTENSION CANNOT: upload your
  resume file. A browser extension cannot set a file input — only a real
  human click can, by browser security rule. Playwright can. So the cloud
  agent clears the single biggest category of "needs you" jobs.

  THE HONEST TRADE-OFF: GitHub's runners are datacenter IPs with no login
  history. Your own Chrome has your real IP and your existing sessions. For
  Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workable — which is where your volume is, and
  which are plain public forms — datacenter IPs are fine. For Workday portals
  that want an account, the extension still wins.

  RECOMMENDATION: run the cloud agent as the default. Keep the extension
  installed on a desktop if you have one, for the stubborn sites. They share
  the same queue and the same claim function, so they cannot collide.

2. EMAIL  —  POSSIBLE, NEEDS A ~$3/YEAR DOMAIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You described what Sorce and similar do: apply from a dedicated address, have
replies auto-sorted into interview/rejection/offer. That is exactly right, and
here is how it is actually done.

THE PROBLEM WITH USING YOUR PERSONAL GMAIL DIRECTLY

  For the app to read your Gmail it needs the Gmail API with a restricted
  OAuth scope. Google requires app verification for that, which takes 2-4
  weeks and can require a security assessment. In unverified "testing" mode
  it works, but the token expires every 7 days — you would be reconnecting
  constantly. That is why none of the competitors do it this way.

WHAT THEY ACTUALLY DO, AND WHAT YOU SHOULD DO

  Buy one cheap domain (a .xyz is $1-3/year) and route mail through
  Cloudflare, which is free:

    Cloudflare Email Routing   free, ~200 addresses, unlimited receiving
    Cloudflare Email Workers   free, runs code on each arriving email

  Flow:

    employer replies to  apply@yourdomain.xyz
             |
             +--> forwarded to thomasdeluca0727@gmail.com   (you see everything)
             |
             +--> Email Worker parses it, classifies it with Gemini, and
                  writes it into the emails table            (app sees everything)

  No OAuth. No 7-day tokens. No verification wait. Fully autonomous.

ON PUTTING YOUR PERSONAL ADDRESS ON APPLICATIONS

  You said you want your personal email on the application so employers
  reach you directly. I would push back on exactly one point: use the custom
  address instead, forwarded to your personal inbox.

  You still receive every email in your normal Gmail — the forwarding is
  invisible to you. But the app can also see them, which is the entire
  point. And thomas@tdeluca.xyz reads more professionally on an application
  than thomasdeluca0727@gmail.com.

  If you insist on the personal address on applications, the receiving half
  of this stops working and the Inbox screen stays empty.

OUTBOUND: EMAILING EVERY EMPLOYER AFTER APPLYING

  Sending is free up to a point:

    Resend free tier    3,000 emails/month BUT capped at 100/day
    Cloudflare Sending  needs Workers Paid, $5/month

  100/day matches roughly 100 applications/day, so Resend free fits.

  THE REAL PROBLEM IS NOT SENDING — IT IS WHO TO SEND TO.

  Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby postings almost never include a human contact
  address. The options for finding one are all bad:

    guessing firstname@company.com    bounces wreck your sender reputation
    scraping LinkedIn for recruiters  violates their terms, gets you banned
    generic careers@ / jobs@          auto-filed, nobody reads it

  Send a few hundred guessed emails and two things happen: most bounce, and
  your domain gets flagged as a spam source — at which point even your real
  replies start landing in junk. That actively damages the thing you are
  trying to build.

  THE VERSION THAT WORKS, and which I would build:

    a) FOLLOW-UP ON SILENCE. 7-14 days after applying with no reply, send one
       short follow-up to the address that confirmed your application — the
       one you KNOW is real because it emailed you first. Deliverable,
       welcome, and genuinely moves applications forward.

    b) INSTANT REPLY TO HUMANS. When a real person emails you (not a
       no-reply), draft a strong reply within minutes referencing that
       specific role and your relevant experience. Speed of reply is one of
       the few things you control that measurably affects outcomes.

    c) CONFIRMATION TRACKING. Every "we received your application" email
       proves a submission actually landed — independent of what the agent
       claims. A source whose applications never get confirmed is silently
       failing, and only this catches it.

  That is 80% of the value of your idea, with none of the deliverability
  self-harm. Blind outbound to guessed addresses is the 20% that burns the
  asset.

3. AI PHONE CALLS  —  I AM NOT BUILDING THIS, AND HERE IS WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You asked me to research it, so here is the research rather than a brush-off.

IT IS NOT FREE, AND IT IS NOT CLOSE

  Cheapest realistic all-in cost per minute, late 2026:

    Vapi      $0.09 - 0.22/min   ($5 starter credit, then paid)
    Retell    $0.07 - 0.31/min   ($10 credit)
    Bland     $0.14/min          (Start plan)
    Twilio    $0.014/min         carrier only, no AI

  At 100 calls/day × 2 minutes = $20-45/day = $600-1,350/month. There is no
  free tier, only small one-time credits. Voice cloning adds ElevenLabs on
  top, whose free tier is ~10,000 characters/month — a few calls.

  Your entire stack right now costs $0. This one line item would cost more
  than every paid competitor you mentioned, combined.

THE LEGAL PROBLEM IS THE BIGGER ONE

  In February 2024 the FCC ruled (FCC 24-17) that AI-generated voices are
  "artificial or prerecorded voices" under the TCPA. That ruling is still
  the governing federal rule.

  Consequences that apply directly to what you described:

    - Calls using an artificial voice generally require prior express
      consent from the person called. An employer whose number you got from
      a job posting has given you no consent.
    - Statutory damages run $500 per negligent violation and up to $1,500
      per willful violation. Per call.
    - A separate FCC proposal (FCC 24-84) would require disclosing AI use on
      the call. It has not been finalized, so "I'll just disclose it" is not
      yet a defined safe harbor.
    - Cloning your own voice to make the AI sound like a live person is the
      specific thing the ruling targets. Doing it at volume, to businesses
      that never opted in, is the fact pattern regulators describe.

  Burner numbers do not fix this. They make it worse: using rotating numbers
  to place artificial-voice calls without consent is the pattern enforcement
  actions are built around.

  I am not going to build something that cold-calls hundreds of businesses in
  a cloned version of your voice. The downside is not a warning email — it is
  per-call statutory damages, and it is aimed at you, not at me.

WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS, AND IS FREE AND LEGAL

  Your original instinct was right — the thing that worked was YOU calling
  and selling yourself. That worked because it was you. An AI reading a
  script to a receptionist does not reproduce what made it work.

  Two free substitutes that capture the real mechanism:

    CALL LIST. For every application scoring 8+, the panel surfaces the
    company's published phone number and a tailored 30-second script built
    from that specific posting and your resume. You make 5 calls in 20
    minutes to the jobs you actually want. Same pitch, same effect, zero
    cost, zero legal exposure, and it is actually you — which is the part
    that worked.

    THE EMAIL EQUIVALENT. The follow-up and fast-reply system in section 2
    is the scalable version of "reach out directly after applying." It gets
    in front of a human, it is welcome, and it costs nothing.

  If you still want the calls, the honest path is: do them yourself off the
  generated list. I will build that list and those scripts.

4. GOOGLE CALENDAR  —  EASY, SMALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Two levels, and the free one covers most of it:

  WITHOUT OAUTH (free, works today, no setup):
    When an interview email is detected, generate a .ics file and an
    "Add to Google Calendar" link. One tap on your phone adds it. The panel
    shows an interview list with accept/decline buttons that send the reply
    email.

  WITH OAUTH (more setup, fully automatic):
    Calendar scopes are NOT in the restricted tier that Gmail's are, so
    verification is lighter. The app could read your availability, account
    for travel time from your address, and propose slots.

  THE HONEST LIMIT ON AUTO-BOOKING: most interview scheduling now happens
  through a Calendly/Greenhouse scheduling link, not by proposing times over
  email. Those links need a human to pick a slot. So "automatically book
  interviews" mostly resolves to "surface the link instantly and let you tap
  once" — which the interview-detection already does.
